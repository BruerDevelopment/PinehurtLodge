## 5. S. Boulder Creek 

**Location:** Accessed from pull-offs along E. Portal Road (County Road 16) or Boulder Canyon Road (CO-119).<br/>
**Fish Species:** Rainbow trout, brown trout, and brook trout.

Welcome to S. Boulder Creek, a beloved fishing spot nestled in the heart of Boulder Canyon, Colorado. Accessible from pull-offs along E. Portal Road and Boulder Canyon Road, this picturesque stream is renowned for its diverse trout population, including rainbow, brown, and brook trout. While the Front Range location makes it a popular destination, especially during early spring and late summer, the fishing experience varies with the water flow. During the peak run-off in May and June, water levels can be too high for ideal fishing conditions. For a more remote and tranquil experience, head to the E. Portal Access, where the creek meanders through serene landscapes. Whether you're a seasoned angler or a novice looking to cast your line, S. Boulder Creek offers a rewarding fishing experience amidst Colorado's stunning natural beauty.

## 4. Big Thompson River

**Location:** Moraine Park in Rocky Mountain National Park.<br/>
**Fish Species:** Smaller brookies and brown trout, fewer rainbows.<br/>

Here, anglers can expect to encounter smaller brook trout and brown trout, with fewer rainbows in the mix. The river winds gently through an expansive moraine, creating a serene setting punctuated by willow glades and calm stretches of water. For successful fishing, anglers are advised to use emergers and nymphs, though dry flies can prove effective during late summer when the fish are more active near the surface. Whether you're a seasoned angler or new to the sport, the Big Thompson River offers a picturesque backdrop and abundant opportunities to reel in your next catch amidst the natural beauty of Moraine Park.

## 3. Gross Reservoir

**Location:** Accessible from Coal Creek Canyon Road (CO-72)<br/>
**Fish Species:** Rainbow trout, brown trout, and kokanee salmon. Maybe some tiger muskie present as well.<br/>

Gross Reservoir, nestled at an elevation of 7,225 feet (2,202 m) and accessible via Coal Creek Canyon Road (CO-72), is a pristine mountain oasis. Anglers are drawn here for its abundant fishing opportunities, which include rainbow trout, brown trout, and kokanee salmon. There's even a chance to hook into a tiger muskie, adding excitement to your angling adventure. The reservoir's scenic beauty is unparalleled, offering stunning mountain vistas as you cast your line. Whether you prefer shore fishing or exploring the waters by boat, Gross Reservoir promises a memorable fishing experience amidst Colorado's natural splendor.

## 2. Clear Creek Canyon

**Location:** Accessed from pull-offs on US-6 through Clear Creek Canyon<br/>
**Fish Species:** Brown trout, rainbow trout, a few brookies.<br/>

Welcome to Clear Creek Canyon, a haven for anglers accessible via pull-offs along US-6. This picturesque canyon is renowned for its thriving fishery, boasting populations of brown trout, rainbow trout, and occasional brook trout. The canyon's deep-cut walls create a serene backdrop as you cast your line into its clear, rushing waters. Thanks to consistent water flow and favorable conditions, Clear Creek Canyon offers productive fishing opportunities nearly year-round. Whether you're wading in its cool currents or casting from the rocky shores, this scenic stretch of Clear Creek promises an enjoyable and fruitful fishing experience for anglers of all skill levels.

## 1. High Mountain Lakes
**Location:** Accessed from the Moffat Tunnel trailhead into the James Peak Wilderness.<br/>
**Fish Species:** Rainbow trout.<br/>

The pristine high-mountain lakes near Rollinsville, tucked away along the eastern side of the Continental Divide. These secluded gems, such as Crater Lake, Rogers Pass Lake, and Heart Lake, are stocked with vibrant rainbow trout courtesy of Colorado Parks and Wildlife. Accessed from the Moffat Tunnel trailhead into the James Peak Wilderness, these lakes offer anglers a truly immersive fishing adventure amidst some of the highest peaks in the country. While reaching these lakes demands a moderate to strenuous hike of up to 4 miles one way, the journey is rewarded with unparalleled solitude and the chance to fish in untouched, alpine surroundings. Whether you're casting from the shore or exploring by boat, these high-mountain lakes promise a quintessential Colorado fishing experience that's both challenging and deeply rewarding.

## **Tips for Fishing in Rollinsville:**
- **Seasonal Considerations:** 
    - **-** Check seasonal fishing regulations. [Check Fishing Season Dates & License Fees](https://cpw.state.co.us/thingstodo/Pages/FishingSeasonDatesFees.aspx)
    - **-** Run-off from the peaks swells creeks in the area to almost unfishable levels between late April and late June.
    - **-** High-mountain lakes are usually not accessible until after June 21st due to late season snow drifts and the risk of trap-door conditions.
    - **-** Early mornings and late evenings can be very cool during the warmer months, with temps falling into the high 30s on some nights in the high elevations, so layers are always recommended, no matter the time of year.
- **Gear Up:** 
    - **-** Pack appropriate gear including rods, reels, and a variety of lures or flies.
    - **-** Always bring rain gear along when fishing in the mountains. The weather can change quickly and drastically and temperatures can swing by more than 10 degrees during storms. 
- **Respect Nature:** 
    - **-** Always practice Leave No Trace principles and respect wildlife by keeping your distance while enjoying these pristine fishing spots.
